Operating Systems - Demo Knowledge

A process is a program in execution.

A thread is the smallest unit of CPU execution within a process. Multiple
threads belonging to the same process share resources such as memory,
while each thread has its own stack and program counter.

A race condition occurs when multiple threads access shared data
concurrently and the final result depends on the order of execution.

A critical section is a part of a program where shared resources are
accessed. Mutual exclusion ensures that only one process or thread
executes a critical section at a time.

A deadlock is a situation where processes are permanently waiting for
resources held by each other.

The four necessary conditions for deadlock are mutual exclusion,
hold and wait, no preemption, and circular wait.

Synchronization mechanisms such as mutexes, semaphores, and monitors
can be used to coordinate concurrent processes and threads.
